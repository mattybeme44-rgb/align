Align / field reference

Listen with your eyes.

A practical guide to measuring loudspeakers, comparing a main and subwoofer, and reading the result without mistaking a preview for a hardware adjustment.

All processing runs on your device. Keep this manual handy during setup; it also prints cleanly to PDF from a browser.

Web edition: open Align in Safari on the iPhone (or another browser that allows microphone access), press Start and allow the microphone. A browser inside another app may block audio, so use Safari. Captures and presets are kept in this browser's local storage, not in Files and not synced to another device.

1. Start safely

Minimum: an iPhone microphone and the phone's output can demonstrate the workflow. For trustworthy timing: use a calibrated measurement mic, a two-input USB-C interface, and a reference loopback from the same signal feeding the speaker. The phone's own input and output have latency, so generated-reference delay on the phone is best treated as a relative comparison, not an absolute distance.

  1. Connect the microphone and audio interface before tapping Start. If you plug in a new interface while running, tap Restart under Input.
  2. Turn down the speaker or interface first. Select Pink noise or another test signal in Source, raise Level slowly and watch for the red clipping warning. Never put a mic in front of a loud speaker at high volume.
  3. Under Source, choose Generated reference for one input, or Input 2 (loopback) when an interface supplies a reference on a separate channel. Select the actual measurement and reference inputs; they must not be the same channel.
  4. Place the microphone at the intended position and keep it still. Wait for the trace and coherence to settle. Use Find delay if the arrival lies outside the short tracking window.

Volume safety: the app can produce a continuous test signal. Start quiet, avoid feedback, and stop the measurement before changing the wiring.

2. Read the measurement screen

Input

Input levels, device, sample rate and clip warning. Large SPL meter opens a focused level readout.

Transfer function

Magnitude in dB, phase in degrees, coherence and a movable frequency cursor.

Impulse response

Arrival at 0 ms and later reflections. Display can be held or refreshed every 0.5, 1 or 2 seconds.

Traces

Quick Capture A/B, phase preview delay, peak hold, average and the Capture library.

Magnitude shows level versus frequency. The curve can be normalized to the 300 Hz–3 kHz band, so its dB numbers are then relative rather than SPL. Phase tells you frequency-dependent timing, not loudness. Coherence shows whether a phase or magnitude reading is repeatable; gaps below 35% are intentionally not trusted. Tap the transfer plot for frequency, magnitude, phase and phase differences; drag across it to zoom, then Reset.

The Input tile and large meter show dBFS until you turn on dB SPL and enter a known calibration offset in Analysis. A phone mic is not automatically an SPL-calibrated measurement microphone. The spectrograph setting shows a scrolling microphone spectrum; the Spectrogram opened under Impulse response is instead a heat map of impulse decay.

Impulse response is a time-domain arrival, not a moving oscilloscope waveform. The strongest peak is placed at 0 ms, with reflections afterward. Hold freezes only its display; meters and analysis stay live. Drag to zoom; gate the impulse response to restrict the magnitude transform to a selected time window. A very short gate sacrifices low-frequency resolution.

3. Main + sub alignment

A timing preview helps you decide what to change in an external speaker processor. Align does not send the preview delay to your subwoofer, main speaker or interface.

  1. Use one measurement mic position and a common reference. In Analysis, turn Compensate delay in phase OFF before taking either capture: otherwise the measured arrival delay has already been removed from the plotted phase.
  2. Run only the main speaker and let the measurement settle. Tap Capture A in Traces; optionally name and save A as “Main left / front row” in the Capture library.
  3. Run only the subwoofer, without moving the mic or changing the reference, and tap Capture B. You can also leave B live instead of capturing it. The phase toggles for A/B are enabled when you capture.
  4. Drag across the transfer chart to zoom around the crossover (for example 60–120 Hz). Compare the two phase traces and check coherence before drawing conclusions.
  5. Use the Capture A/B phase delay slider or ±0.1 ms buttons to preview timing. Positive milliseconds delay that captured speaker; negative milliseconds advance it. The cursor's Δ phase changes along with the plotted phase, while magnitude and the stored raw capture remain unchanged.
  6. Make the corresponding change in the loudspeaker DSP, then measure again. Check the combined response with both speakers on, not just phase at one frequency: polarity, crossover filters and room reflections also matter.

Do not confuse controls: Recommended / Apply and the Analysis Offset affect the measurement's reference alignment. Capture preview delay rotates a stored phase curve only; it is not a hardware delay and it does not modify the recorded trace.

4. Capture library

Open Traces → Capture library. Choose Live, Capture A or Capture B, tap Save capture, enter a meaningful label and Save. A and B can be saved while held, even after the live trace stops. New saves keep magnitude, sampled phase and coherence, original delay, settings and any preview delay. Older saved measurements remain available but carry magnitude only.

Saved captures are kept in this browser's local storage, not automatically exported to Files or synced to another phone. There is no automatic 20-capture deletion: items remain until you use Delete, or clear the browser's site data. Back up important work separately with the Share actions.

Tap a row to toggle its overlay; select up to eight at once, then Done to return to the chart. Each gets its own color and label. With phase enabled, newer captures also show a dashed phase overlay; use each active overlay's slider in Traces to save a preview delay for that specific capture. Remove an overlay without deleting its saved record. Clear all overlays from the library. Search, rename, add notes or delete. Saving a preview delay changes only the overlay setting: no processor or audio routing is modified.

Important: the library's stored phase is a compact frequency-sampled copy, not the original microphone audio or impulse response. A saved curve cannot reconstruct room decay. For an archive outside the app, use CSV, image or PDF sharing while the original live measurement is still available.

5. Analysis tools

FFT, averaging and smoothing

FFT size controls how narrowly frequencies can be resolved: a larger size takes longer and asks for a steadier signal, especially at low frequencies. Averaging makes the response settle more slowly but less noisily. Octave smoothing makes magnitude easier to read; it cannot repair a poor microphone position or low coherence.

Delay and impulse

Find delay locks a coarse arrival after a long probe. A manually entered Offset moves the analysis reference. The Recommended tile compares the live arrival with A or B; Apply moves that capture's stored arrival onto the live one for the comparison and leaves the audio path untouched. The impulse gate can reject late reflections in the transfer magnitude; the Waterfall and impulse-decay Spectrogram show how energy continues after the direct arrival. The Reflections card lists strong early peaks; Room decay estimates an RT60 only where the energy envelope has a believable slope.

Levels and signal checks

Check noise silences the source briefly and measures the noise floor. The SPL meter uses the selected microphone input's level, not the transfer function level. The calibration offset is a user-supplied number for your complete mic/interface gain chain; without it, use dBFS. If the Input card reports clipping, lower source or interface gain before trusting a measurement.

Other views

Target curve adds a reference for magnitude deviations. Band table lists third-octave level, target difference and coherence. Group delay derives timing from the slope of phase and omits low-coherence bins. Peak hold and Capture average are temporary analysis tools that reset on restart; save important A/B captures to the library.

Presets

Presets keep the FFT size, averaging, smoothing, gate, signal, level and reference under a name, so a repeatable setup is one tap away. Open Presets from Analysis, save the current settings, then Apply a preset to put them back on the measurement. You can search by name or note, add or edit a note, rename, duplicate or delete. Up to 20 are kept in this browser's local storage, newest first.

6. Share and export

The Share card sends a concise text summary, a chart image with visible overlays and legends, a full per-bin CSV or a one-page PDF report. On a phone that offers file sharing the browser's share sheet opens; otherwise the file downloads. On iPhone this works best in Safari, and the download lands in Files. The saved-capture library is private app data; sharing a chart is not the same thing as backing up the library. These export actions require a current measurement, even when old captures remain in the library.

7. Troubleshooting

Nothing appears

Grant mic permission, press Start, confirm the route and signal level; Demo measurement tests the interface without a microphone.

Phase is unreliable

Check coherence and loopback. Turn off phase compensation before both main/sub captures. Recapture after any routing or setting change.

Delay jumps

Check routing and keep the phone/mic still. Use a cable and loopback reference; Find delay again if the source moves.

No SPL calibration

Leave the meter in dBFS until you have a calibrated mic and know the offset for its complete gain chain.
